A guide to the highlights

Document prediction

The selected model classifies the document using its existing input policy. The original models use a 512-token lexical window; RACE also processes full-document discourse structure.

Passage highlights

The highlights show the model’s predictions for individual passages. They are not verified authorship labels or an explanation of the document-level prediction. Up to eight passages are sent separately to the same model, and a color shows that passage’s top predicted category.

Inspect a selection

Select text in the Inspect view, then choose Inspect selection. Scores apply only to the submitted excerpt. The document prediction does not change.

Coverage & discourse

Coverage uses the actual lexical character boundary returned by the server. Discourse units show parser boundaries, not AI-generated spans. Uninspected or truncated text is not given a category color.

Four writing categories

Human-written; human-written and AI-polished; AI-generated; AI-created and subsequently rewritten by a person or tool. These are dataset labels, not a verified writing history.

Privacy & limits

Text is sent to your local service. The page does not persist documents or call external services. Scores are not calibrated authorship probabilities. More highlights mean more inference requests.

About this page

A local reading studio for the EE6405 text-provenance project. Everything runs on your machine.

What it does

Classifies an English document into four writing categories and shows which parts of the text drove the view.

How to use it

Paste or import text, pick a detection model, then choose Analyze document. Passage highlights follow automatically.

Four models

TF-IDF, RoBERTa, RACE and RACE without contrastive loss. Each is an independent classifier; scores are never fused.

Reading the output

Whole-document scores sit on the right. Passage, coverage and discourse views explain the input, not the label.

Privacy

Text goes only to the local service on this machine. Nothing is uploaded, stored or sent to a third party.

Limits

Scores are learned patterns, not calibrated authorship probabilities or proof of who wrote the text.
